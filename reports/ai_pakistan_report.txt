RESEARCH REPORT
===============

TITLE
Development Status of Artificial Intelligence in Pakistan

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
------------------
Pakistan is actively advancing its artificial intelligence ecosystem through strategic government initiatives, policy frameworks, and a transitioning startup landscape. The federal cabinet approved the National Artificial Intelligence Policy 2025 in July 2025 to create an innovation-driven and ethical AI environment. Despite these milestones and a growing tech talent pool, Pakistan faces challenges in infrastructure, legal frameworks for data protection, and regional AI readiness standings.

KEY FINDINGS
------------
1. The federal cabinet unanimously approved the National Artificial Intelligence Policy 2025 on July 30, 2025.
2. The 2025 AI Policy features a six-pillar national roadmap, establishing structures such as an AI Council and the National AI Fund.
3. Pakistan's AI startup ecosystem is transitioning away from a heavy outsourcing model toward product development and AI-native startups.
4. On the Oxford Insights Government AI Readiness Index, Pakistan ranks 8th out of 17 in South and Central Asia, placing it behind regional neighbors like India, Bangladesh, and Sri Lanka due to infrastructure and implementation gaps.
5. The country faces ongoing hurdles concerning outdated data protection laws and cybersecurity frameworks.

DETAILED ANALYSIS
-----------------
Pakistan's artificial intelligence landscape is undergoing a notable transition. At the institutional level, the government has taken significant steps, highlighted by the Ministry of Planning, Development, and Special Initiatives (MoPDSI) convening a National AI Taskforce meeting in January 2025 to advance the AI Implementation Roadmap. This culminated in the federal cabinet's unanimous approval of the National Artificial Intelligence Policy 2025 on July 30, 2025. The policy establishes a comprehensive six-pillar roadmap designed to foster an inclusive, secure, and innovation-driven AI ecosystem, supported by institutional mechanisms like the AI Council and funding tools such as the National AI Fund (NAIF). Simultaneously, the commercial and startup sectors are evolving. Driven by increasing venture capital interest and a growing tech talent pool, Pakistan's ecosystem is shifting from an outsourcing-heavy operational model toward product development and AI-native startups. However, structural and systemic challenges remain. According to the 2025 Government AI Readiness Index by Oxford Insights, Pakistan ranks 8th out of 17 in South and Central Asia, trailing countries such as India, Bangladesh, and Sri Lanka. This lower ranking underscores persistent gaps in digital infrastructure, data systems, and public sector adoption. Furthermore, legal challenges, including outdated data protection laws and the absence of robust mechanisms to govern ethical dilemmas and cybersecurity threats, continue to pose hurdles to sustainable AI growth.

CONCLUSION
----------
Pakistan is demonstrating strong intent and momentum in developing its AI sector through formal policies like the National AI Policy 2025 and an evolving startup landscape. While government frameworks and taskforces are actively paving the way for digital transformation, closing infrastructural gaps, improving regional readiness rankings, and updating legal data protection mechanisms are vital for long-term success.
